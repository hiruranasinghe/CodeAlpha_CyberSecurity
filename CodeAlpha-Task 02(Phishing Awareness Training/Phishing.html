🎣

Phishing

Mass email campaigns impersonating banks, tech companies, or agencies to steal credentials or install malware on your device.

🎯

Spear Phishing

Targeted attacks tailored to a specific person using personal details gathered from social media and public records.

🐳

Whaling

Spear phishing aimed at executives - one compromised CEO account can authorise fraudulent wire transfers worth millions.

📱

Smishing / Vishing

Phishing via SMS (smishing) or phone calls (vishing), often impersonating banks, delivery services or tech support.

Module 02

How to Spot a Phishing Attack

Every phishing attempt leaves traces - train yourself to see them before it's too late.

  • ✕
    Suspicious sender address"PayPal Support" in the display name but the actual address is paypal-security@gmail-support.net. Always expand the sender field to verify the real email address.
  • ✕
    Urgency and threats"Your account will be suspended in 24 hours!" Attackers manufacture panic to short-circuit your rational thinking. Legitimate companies don't threaten immediate account closure.
  • ✕
    Unexpected attachmentsMalicious .docx, .pdf, or .zip files often contain hidden macros or executables. Never open attachments you didn't request - even from people you know.
  • !
    Deceptive or mismatched URLsHover before you click. paypaI.com (capital I) ≠ paypal.com. Attackers use homoglyph attacks, fake subdomains (paypal.login.evil.com), and URL shorteners to hide real destinations.
  • !
    Generic greetings"Dear Customer" instead of your actual name signals a mass campaign. Your bank and every service you use knows your name - they always use it.
  • !
    Poor grammar or spellingErrors can indicate a non-native attacker, but don't rely on this alone - sophisticated attacks are now grammatically flawless thanks to AI tools.
  • ✓
    Requests for sensitive informationLegitimate organisations will NEVER ask for your password, PIN, or full credit card number via email. If they ask - it's a phish.

Module 04

Social Engineering Tactics

Attackers exploit human psychology — not just technology. Understanding these tactics is your best defence.

Key insight: Social engineering bypasses firewalls, antivirus, and every technical control by targeting the human. No technology protects against a person willingly handing over their credentials.
😨

Fear & Urgency

"Your account will be deleted." Panic short-circuits careful thinking. Attackers deliberately compress your decision window so you act before you think.

🏅

Authority

Impersonating a CEO, IT dept, IRS, or police. People are conditioned to comply with authority - attackers exploit this reflex to bypass normal scepticism.

🎁

Greed & Reward

"You've won an iPhone!" Offers too good to be true always are. Prize-claim pages harvest your credentials and payment details.

🤝

Trust & Rapport

Attackers may spend weeks building trust before making their ask. Spear phishers reference real names, projects, and colleagues to seem credible.

👥

Social Proof

"Your colleagues already updated their details." Implying consensus lowers resistance — you don't want to be the only one who hasn't complied.

🎭

Pretexting

"I'm from IT, upgrading your account - I need your password." Always verify requests through official channels, never through contact details the caller provides.

Module 05

Protecting Yourself

Practical, actionable defences you can implement right now — no technical background required.

🔐

Enable MFA Everywhere

Multi-factor authentication blocks 99.9% of credential attacks even if your password is stolen. Use an authenticator app — not SMS — where possible.

🔗

Verify URLs Manually

Never click email links. Type the address directly into your browser or use saved bookmarks. HTTPS doesn't guarantee legitimacy — attackers use it too.

📞

Call to Confirm

If an email demands action, call the organisation using the number on their official website - never a number in the email itself.

🗝️

Use a Password Manager

Password managers auto-fill only on the correct domain. On a spoof site, it won't fill acting as a built-in phishing detector.

🚩

Report It Immediately

Hit "Report Phishing" in your email client and tell your IT team. Speed matters - others may have the same email in their inbox right now.

🧠

Pause Before You Click

If an email made you anxious, excited, or curious - that's intentional. Those emotions are the weapon. Take 30 seconds to verify before acting.

Golden rule: When in doubt, throw it out. Deleting a legitimate email is a minor inconvenience. Clicking one malicious link can compromise your entire organisation's network.
